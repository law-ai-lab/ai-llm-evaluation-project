# Unsupported Claims

## Definition

An unsupported claim is presented as factual, but no reliable evidence can be found to support or contradict it.

## Key Distinction

An unsupported claim is not automatically inaccurate.

- **Inaccurate:** Reliable evidence contradicts the claim.
- **Unsupported:** No reliable evidence supports or contradicts the claim.
- **Cannot confidently assess:** The claim cannot be evaluated because essential context or historical evidence is unavailable.

## Common Warning Signs

- A business is said to offer a service that is not mentioned anywhere
- A person is credited with an achievement that cannot be verified
- A specific feature is attributed to a product without documentation
- A claim sounds plausible but lacks any reliable source
- Search results repeat the claim without identifying an original source

## Example AI Response

> Tanuki’s Cave offers catering for weddings, private parties, and corporate events.

## Claim Decomposition

1. The restaurant offers catering.
2. It provides catering for weddings.
3. It provides catering for private parties.
4. It provides catering for corporate events.

## Evidence Needed

- Official restaurant website
- Official social media accounts
- Catering menu or booking page
- Reliable local business directory
- Direct announcement from the restaurant

## Evaluation

**Rating:** Unsupported

## Explanation

The official website discusses dining, menus, and reservations but does not mention catering. It also does not explicitly state that catering is unavailable. Therefore, there is no reliable evidence supporting or contradicting the claim.

## Improved Response

> The restaurant’s official website does not mention catering services. Users should contact the restaurant directly to confirm whether catering is available.

## Common Evaluator Mistake

Do not rate a claim Inaccurate only because it is absent from the official website. Silence is not direct contradiction.

## Core Principle

**Lack of supporting evidence is not the same as evidence that a claim is false.**
