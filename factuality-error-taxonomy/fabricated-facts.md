# Fabricated Facts

## Definition

A fabricated fact is a person, event, study, quotation, statistic, organization, or detail presented as real without reliable supporting evidence.

## Common Warning Signs

- Highly specific statistics without a source
- Studies with no identifiable title or authors
- Invented quotations
- Organizations or programs that cannot be verified
- Detailed claims that sound plausible but have no evidence

## Example AI Response

> A 2025 Oxford University study involving 12,000 adults found that drinking two cups of green tea daily improved memory by 31%.

## Claim Decomposition

1. A study was published in 2025.
2. Oxford University conducted the study.
3. The study involved 12,000 adults.
4. Participants drank two cups of green tea daily.
5. Memory performance improved by 31%.

## Evidence Needed

- Oxford University research records
- Academic database results
- Original study or publication
- Author names
- Sample size and methodology
- Reported numerical findings

## Evaluation

**Rating:** Inaccurate, if thorough research confirms that the study does not exist.

## Explanation

The response presents a highly specific academic study and numerical result, but no reliable institutional or academic source confirms the study, sample size, intervention, or reported improvement.

## Improved Response

> Some studies have examined possible relationships between green tea and cognitive health, but the specific Oxford University study and 31% improvement claim could not be verified.

## Common Evaluator Mistake

Do not conclude that a study is fabricated merely because it does not appear on one website. Search the university, academic databases, and the exact study details before assigning a rating.
